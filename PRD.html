SECTION 1

Vision & thesis

Existing tools (WHOOP, Oura) surface generic advice from naive correlations that can’t control for confounds, and their logging friction burns users out — the founder quit WHOOP’s journal after ~250 committed days. CALIBEX’s opening is structural: friction engineered out, and statistical honesty engineered in.

The original thesis (“beat WHOOP with rigorous passive correlation”) was itself stress-tested and reframed: rigorous passive correlation at n-of-1 is structurally underpowered — honest gates may stay closed for months. So the insight engine is inverted:

M1Frictionless capture
M2Honest mirror
M3Deliberate experiments
M4The Playbook
THE DESTINATION

“Late session tonight — what do I eat so sleep isn’t affected?” CALIBEX answers with your tested protocol, because deliberate A/B cycles answered it — not because a correlation guessed.

Passive correlations are demoted to hypothesis generators. They suggest what to experiment on; the experiment delivers the verdict. A deliberate two-week A/B sidesteps the low-n problem entirely — and the founder’s constant daily dinner is an experimental asset: a stable baseline where every deliberate tweak is measurable.

SECTION 3

Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • Primary (scored): performance, body composition, day-to-day recovery.
  • Sustain logging long-term by making capture near-invisible (~2 min rest day / ~3 min training day — the honest promise).
  • Build a compounding personal dataset — even unanswered questions keep their option value for future re-mining.
  • Answer situational questions via the Playbook (“my day changed — what’s my move?”).

Non-goals (explicit)

  • No coaching product, no therapy, no wellness app. It is an instrument.
  • No 1–10 self-rating scales, anywhere. Behavioural/somatic anchors only.
  • No AI photo meal logger — cut as too inaccurate. No streaks, nags, or guilt mechanics; a skip is silent missing data.
  • No surfaced insight below the honesty gate. “Not enough data yet” beats a spurious finding — one fabricated insight destroys the instrument’s credibility.
  • Work-life balance / mental peace are inputs to the sustainability axis — never scored goals.
SECTION 4

The 7-dimension athlete framework

CALIBEX dropped the 6 ACLM lifestyle-medicine pillars (built for population disease-prevention) for a purpose-built athlete-optimization framework — derived from validated athlete-monitoring science (RESTQ-Sport, Hooper, DALDA, Foster load metrics, the Kellmann recovery-stress consensus, ABQ burnout model). The 7-dimension framework is the only framework.

Three outcome axes

A

Next-day recovery

Daily · scored (M3)

Behavioural inputs correlated against tomorrow’s WHOOP HRV / recovery / RHR / sleep. The daily loop.

B

Sustainability / burnout

Weekly · observational flag

A WHOOP-invisible early-warning trend from self-reported signals (detachment, autonomy, devaluation). Never correlated against WHOOP — its outcome is the self-reported trend itself.

C

Progress toward goals

Weekly/monthly · trend display

Is it working? Per-goal trends (strength, endurance, body-comp). Input→progress correlation deferred until data clears honest gating.

WIRING DISCIPLINE

Life-Demand Load and Social each play two roles — an Axis-B trend term and an Axis-A multiplier on training cost (recovery_dip = f(load) × life_demand_modifier) — but are never double-counted as additive daily strain. The three axes never silently correlate weekly-against-weekly at low n.

SECTION 5

The instrument — final question set

~27 Axis-A items + 12 Axis-B items, sampled so the user only ever sees a few per touchpoint. Every item passes the memorability rule: it must reference a salient event, an action-with-consequence, or the current state at the moment asked — if the only way to answer is to guess, the item is cut. Severity 0–1, higher = more strain; protective items score asymmetrically (a “No” is neutral, never max strain); conditional items score N/A, never 0.

SAMPLER — HARD REQUIREMENTS

Least-recently-served, dimension-stratified rotation with a per-facet staleness ceiling — and every dinner sample opens on a protective item. With ~14 items in the dinner pool, these two rules are what keep fill-rates viable and the evening review from becoming a deficit-sweep. They are MVP requirements, not polish.

Honest daily cost: ~12–14 one-taps + a short per-session confirm ≈ 2 min rest day / 3 min training day, never in one sitting.

#1 PRODUCT RISK

Logging into a void. WHOOP at least paid a daily score; a purely silent CALIBEX gives back nothing for a month — strictly worse than the thing that already burned the founder out. Mitigation: the raw readout screen is real data (never a faked score) and doubles as the experiment’s instrumentation. One screen, two jobs.

  1. M1 · THIS WEEKCapture v0. Touchpoint capture + raw storage + sampler + readout + weekly review. No scoring views.
  2. M2 · ~4 WEEKSAdherence shakedown (the silent phase). Friction map per touchpoint; per-item variance by eye; hand-prune. Success = sustained logging without it becoming a chore + a prune/keep shortlist.
  3. M3Experiments tab first (primary insight engine — lightweight tag-a-window → baseline-vs-intervention compare), then the scoring engine, lift templates & dish library if data justifies, Goals, Axis-B flag, RED-S guard.
  4. M4Validated instrument + the Playbook. Self-weighting/pruning live; per-deviation protocols accumulating; Axis-C correlations un-deferred once n clears honest gating.
SECTION 7

Flagship features

Experiments — the primary insight engine

Structured n-of-1 A/B on your own body: define an intervention + hypothesis + baseline-vs-intervention window + outcome metrics (HRV, recovery, sleep, subjective). A deliberate two-week A/B is statistically cleaner than passive correlation at low n — it sidesteps the core n-problem. Passive correlations feed it as hypothesis generators.

ARCHITECTURE — SEPARATE FLOW

Experiments live in their own dedicated flow, architecturally separate from the daily capture + scoring loop. The daily loop is passive, quiet, habitual; an experiment is active, intentional, and focused (set it up → run it → read the verdict). It must not clutter the everyday capture surface, and where an experiment needs finer data (e.g. intraday HR via Apple Watch), that heavier sampling lives inside the experiment, not the daily ritual.

The Playbook — the product destination (M4)

Per-deviation-type personal protocols accumulated from experiment results: late session · late dinner · travel · drinking night · high-stress day → “here’s what worked for you.” This answers the founder’s flagship question — “late gym tonight, what do I eat so sleep isn’t affected?” — with tested protocols (“lighter, earlier-carb dinner protected your sleep in 3 of 3 trials”), not guesses. Interactions like timing×food are unanswerable passively at n-of-1; a constant dinner means zero variance and nothing to correlate. Only deliberate tweaks answer “what if.”

Intraday biometrics — slicing the data you already have

A device records minute-level heart rate 24/7 — an objective second channel that costs zero extra logging and timestamps what you self-report. Source matters (verified against the real data): WHOOP's official API and its CSV export are daily/per-workout summaries only — no minute-level HR (confirmed in the repo's WHOOP export). Minute HR comes from Apple Watch → HealthKit (the reliable route), or WHOOP's unofficial internal API (fragile, ToS-gray — fine for a one-off experiment, not a backbone). WHOOP stays the daily-recovery engine. Heart rate, not HRV for this layer (HRV is once-daily everywhere — confirmed: one value per row in the export — and remains the recovery outcome of the daily loop); the intraday win is HR reactivity.

THE MECHANISM — TIMESTAMPED INPUT × HR WINDOW

Logged events carry a time (caffeine 4pm · dinner 8:30pm · session end 7pm) and HR is now per-minute, so each event becomes a mini natural experiment: HR before vs after. This quietly attacks the project's hardest problem — low n. A daily correlation gives ~1 point/day; event→HR-response gives one point per event (dozens of caffeines, meals, sessions a month), each a within-you before/after. Far more power, no HRV required.

THE CATCH — EVENT TIME, NOT LOG TIME

Only works when the timestamp marks when the thing happened, not when you tapped it. Clean for time-stamped events — caffeine/alcohol quick-loggers, the dinner eaten-at tag, WHOOP workout times (already captured). Breaks for retrospective items (“snapped at 2pm, logged at dinner”) → they stay daily-resolution unless logged in real time or given an event-time. Even clean events need before/after controls (posture / walking to get the coffee move HR too) — it's a controlled before/after, not a naive bump-counter.

FREE INPUTS — CALENDAR & EXISTING TIMESTAMPED DATA

The best timestamped inputs are ones you never log at all. Your work calendar already knows when meetings are and who attends — pre-timestamped context, zero friction → correlate against intraday HR for a stress map of your week (a viral WHOOP-calendar “coworker stress leaderboard,” built by reverse-engineering WHOOP for per-minute HR, is live proof of exactly this). Generalizes to any existing timestamped source: calendar, WHOOP workout times, the quick-loggers. Feasibility note: for a personal n-of-1 tool, reverse-engineering WHOOP for minute HR is a tolerable risk (proven to work; just re-run if it breaks) — Apple Watch / HealthKit is the more stable route, ToS risk only bites on commercial redistribution.

Goals MAIN SECTION

User-set targets (weight, muscle, 10K time, waist, lift PR) with value + date — the front-end of Axis-C, turning abstract trends into progress-toward-your-target. Per-goal bars + a plain-language roll-up; no single composite bar (it mixes units and can move against a successful recomposition).

ARCHITECTURE — IN THE MAIN VIEW

Goals & progress are woven seamlessly into the app's main section, not a buried side tab. They're the “why am I doing this every day” anchor — visible alongside the daily flow so the logging always connects to the targets it serves. (Opposite of Experiments, which is deliberately its own separate flow.)

Quiet inputs

  • Quick-loggers: alcohol (pegs; default none) and caffeine (cups + last-cup time; pre-filled usual). Logged only when they happen — never asked.
  • Saved templates: workouts (load last session, log only the weight-bumps) and dishes (one-tap repeat + portion) — both M3.
  • Deferred levers: breathwork logger (the one lever with meta-analytic evidence for directly raising vagal HRV — log → correlate → prescribe), weather/AQI auto-context.
SECTION 8

Scoring engine — the M3 machinery

Survived three statistical stress-test rounds. None of it is MVP; all of it is specified:

  • Item-level baselining → trailing 7–14d window → dimension scores. Each item is normalized against its own history before aggregation, so rotating items with different base rates stays valid.
  • Partial-pooling cold-start: shrink each item toward its facet pool until it has enough observations; gate on item maturity — never a silent NULL or raw mean.
  • Protective aggregation: base = mean over answered negative items; a protective “Yes” applies a bounded downward adjustment; a facet is never scored from a protective item alone.
  • Conditional N/A: excluded from gates and baseline denominators (never scored 0).
  • Foster monotony/strain from RPE × duration, scored vs the user’s own baseline (population risk-zones misread a near-daily trainer). ACWR is display-only.
  • Substances off the z-path: absolute dose; is_drinking_day threshold drives exclusions. Never additively pooled with z-scored dimensions.
  • Effective-sample-size gating everywhere: autocorrelated days are not independent; insights gate on n_eff and minimum occurrences, with FDR control across the hypothesis family.
  • Axis-C metrics: est-1RM (Epley, ≤8–10 reps, per-lift), body weight 7-day moving average, waist 4-week fixed-protocol trend, pace-at-HR (heat-flagged).
  • RED-S guard (cross-axis): sustained under-fuelling + declining recovery + stalling strength — a healthy cut holds strength, RED-S regresses it. Observational; never the clinical label.
SECTION 9

Design principles & guardrails

PrincipleWhat it forbids / requires
Friction is structuralLogging piggybacks on existing moments. No streaks/nags. Skips are silent. Honest promise: ~2–3 min/day.
MemorabilityItems reference recallable events/consequences or current state. No fleeting micro-states, no body-scan prompts (nocebo), no “vs normal” self-comparison.
Deviation from baselineEverything scores relative to the user’s own rolling norm — a late dinner matters vs his normal, not a clock.
Statistical honestyNothing surfaces below the gate. “Not enough signal yet” is a feature. No naive Pearson on autocorrelated series.
Item-level storage, computed scoresLong-format rows; scores live in views and can change without rewriting history. wake_date is the universal join key.
Valence balanceEvery touchpoint pool carries an outward-pointing positive; dinner pins one. The instrument must never become a nightly deficit audit.
Self-testing instrumentLow bar to try a signal, high bar to keep it: flat items retire (~30d), predictive items up-weight. The data decides, not paper review.
SECTION 10

Risks & mitigations

Logging into a void

A silent month gives back less than the product that already burned the founder out. Mitigation: raw readout screen from day 1 — real data beside next-day WHOOP.

Low-n honesty paradox

Rigorous passive correlation may honestly say nothing for months. Mitigation: experiments-first reframe; correlations demoted to hypothesis generators.

WHOOP dependency

The outcome variable is a third-party black box; algorithm updates can masquerade as physiological change. Mitigation: prefer raw HRV/RHR over the composite score where possible; pure read-only source.

Nocebo / measurement reactivity

Prompting an anxiety-prone tracker to scan for symptoms can manufacture them. Mitigation: memorability rule, no body-scans, protective pins, evening review framed outward.

Adherence decay (base rates)

Most self-tracking dies by month two. Mitigation: M2 is explicitly an adherence experiment; the friction map prunes whatever doesn’t earn its place.

Surprising-insight paradox

The most novel insights need the most power. Mitigation: the realistic v1 win is quantifying suspected effects (“alcohol costs 3 days, not 1”) — framed as such, honestly.
